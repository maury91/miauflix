# Miauflix Detailed Changelog

Implementation and upgrade notes. For a shorter overview, see the [release notes](CHANGELOG.md).

## Unreleased

### Features

- Configuration, the first-run wizard, Sources, Storage, the Trakt dialog and QR approval now use the design system.
  - Configuration, the first-run wizard, Sources and Storage share `pages/settings/SettingsLayout`: a back `NavRail` (when there is somewhere to go back to) beside a `ScrollArea`, in one `FocusScope`. `shared/hooks/usePageBack` keeps DOM focus on the page so Back works while a text field holds virtual focus, and ends editing first (the profile editor uses it too). The app logo sits in the corner aligned with the content, as on Home and the profile pages; the centred logo and its header strip, which covered the back rail, are gone from these pages. `shared/ui/logo/LogoBackdrop` draws a solid strip (with a short fade) behind the corner logo on the settings pages, the profile editor and Who's watching, so their content scrolls under it; the pages' rails sit above it, and Who's watching starts its list below it when the list overflows. The corner logo's top and height are now `CORNER_LOGO_TOP` and `CORNER_LOGO_HEIGHT` in `logoGeometry`.
  - Configuration groups are `Surface` sections with a status `Badge`, `Disclosure`s for optional and advanced values, and Save/Test in an `ActionGroup`. Each value kind has its control: `Switch`, `Select`, `NumberField` (with a unit `Select` for sizes and durations), `PasswordField` or `TextField`. The wizard's required steps are a list with the current step marked.
  - Sources rows are a `Switch` named by the source (description, usage, errors and badges as its description), a Test `IconButton` and a `Menu` (Settings, Move up/down, Remove). Provider settings register with the navigation layer instead of mapping DOM focus, and opening them focuses the first field. Advanced setup is a `Disclosure`; Add source opens and focuses it. The indexer catalog uses `TextField`, `Select`, `FilterChip`s and `Badge`s; Torznab and repositories use `FormSection`.
  - Storage opens with `storage/StorageUsage`: a bar over the whole filesystem (`filesystem.totalBytes`) split into movies, episodes, anime, unmatched downloads and TV remuxes from the start, then free space, with other data (used space Miauflix doesn't account for) last, and a marker at the configured limit. Video segments are `physicalBytes - remuxBytes` by kind; without filesystem stats the bar spans the larger of the limit and the used bytes. A legend repeats the values in text, and a note gives the bytes charged against the limit, reservations included. `storage/storageInventory` holds `computeUsage`, `sortItems` and `formatBytes`. Items from a backend without `kind` or `remuxBytes` count as unmatched downloads with no remux.
  - Downloads are `ListItem`s with landscape `Artwork` (backdrop, falling back to the episode still or poster), a kind `Badge`, quality, size, status, progress (with a bar while incomplete), file name, reservation and remux size. A `Select` sorts by size, name, type, progress or status. Free space is an `IconButton` that confirms with `Dialog` (Cancel first).
  - A Storage settings card under the bar edits media-load's `STORAGE_THRESHOLD`, `STORAGE_PLAYBACK_RESERVE` and `CONTENT_SEED_DURATION_SECONDS`, with `DOWNLOAD_PATH`, `DOWNLOAD_PATH_EXCLUSIVE` and `STORAGE_PAD_FILE_SIZES` under Advanced (`storage/useStorageSettings`, `storage/StorageSettings`). It reuses `ConfigField` and `useConfigForm`, shows the sizes with a unit `Select` and the seeding time in minutes, hours or days (stored as seconds), and saves only these keys through `PUT /api/config/MEDIA_LOAD`; media-load applies them without a restart. The bar previews an unsaved limit and hatches the playback reserve right after Miauflix's data, so the free space stays one stretch; `ConfigField` puts a size or duration's help text under both the number and its unit, so the unit lines up with the input (in a `div`, since the caption is itself a paragraph); a warning appears when the limit exceeds free space plus what Miauflix holds, or the reserve takes the whole limit. The status note names the saved seeding time. The same values stay on the Configuration page.
  - `StorageItemView` adds `kind` (`movie`, `episode`, `anime` for shows with AniList season layout, `unknown`), `subtitle` (the year, or the episodes such as `S01E02 · Name` or `Season 1 · 3 episodes`), `image` and `remuxBytes`. `ContentSourceRepository.findWithMovies` becomes `findWithMedia` and also loads episodes with their season and show. The `media-load` contract documents `StorageItem.remuxBytes` (since 1.2), which media-load already sent.
  - The Trakt dialog is a `Dialog` with a `PairingPanel` for the code; focus moves to Open Trakt once the code arrives. QR approval is a `Surface` card on its own navigation layer.
  - `layer.page` (1000) names the full-screen pages' z-index. The dialog, tooltip and notification layers move to 1100–1300, above those pages and the app shell's legacy chrome (up to 1002), so a `Dialog` opened from a settings page is no longer drawn behind it.
  - ESLint bans `styled-components` in `pages/{config,qr,settings}` and `features/integrations/ui`. Storybook mocks the config, sources and storage APIs (`__mocks__` with `sb.mock`) for the new Settings stories. The `initial-user-setup` end-to-end spec finds fields by `data-config-field` and the Trakt group by its region; its screenshots need regenerating.

### Developer tooling

- `GlTuning.uploadsPerFrame` (default 2) sets how many decoded images the WebGL renderer uploads per frame; set it on the TV through `localStorage['miauflix:gl-tuning']`. `scripts/image-cost-probe.js`, injected with `scripts/tv-bench.mjs`, times `createImageBitmap` and texture uploads on the main thread. `scripts/tv-bench.mjs` adds `{"viewport": [w, h, dpr]}` and `{"throttle": n}` steps for running the scenarios in desktop Chrome. On the TV, decoding already runs off the main thread and 1, 2 or 4 uploads per frame measured the same; see the [benchmarks](docs/development/tv-benchmarks.md#2026-11-21-image-decoding-and-texture-uploads).

### Bug Fixes

- `NumberField` no longer commits on blur or Enter when the text is unchanged: leaving a field holding a value off its step (`0.125` with step `0.01`) used to save it rounded (`0.13`). Edited text still commits parsed, rounded and clamped.
- The Trakt dialog moves focus to Open Trakt only when the authorization code first arrives; it used to refocus on every render (`useOptionalNavigation()` returns a new object each time), pulling focus off Copy code.
- The QR approval page mounts a new `FocusScope` (with `autoFocus`) for each screen, so Approve, Try again or a profile is focused once the request check finishes instead of nothing until an arrow press.
- The Sources hint renders its caption as a `span`, no longer a `<p>` inside a `<p>`.
- The Storage bar shows "Usage unavailable" instead of "0 B / 0%" when there are no disk stats and a download's size isn't measured (`usedBytes` is null), and a disk reporting a size of 0 no longer shows "NaN%".
- Storybook: mutation mocks resolve like RTK Query triggers (`{ data }` and `unwrap()`, `__test-utils__/mutationTrigger`), so the Sources Test and switches, Storage removal and the wizard's Test and Save show their results instead of errors or a stuck busy state.

## 2026-11-20 v0.3.0 Samsung TV playback and a faster Home

### Features

- Samsung TV builds use PixiJS 8 (WebGL 2) for Home's rows and hero by default. Set `MIAUFLIX_TV_GL_ROWS=false` when running `scripts/package-tizen.sh` to package the DOM renderer as the default. At runtime, `localStorage['miauflix:gl-rows'] = '0'` selects the DOM renderer, and `'1'` enables WebGL on Tizen or in development. The web production bundle uses the DOM renderer and omits the WebGL code.
  - The renderer bakes cards into reusable textures and slides them under a fixed focus ring. The rows keep a DOM representation for accessibility. Pointer hit-testing uses the drawn card bounds, and reduced-motion preferences apply to both the rows and the hero.
  - The renderer redraws after context recovery. A failed load or initialization, a context loss longer than 3 seconds or a third loss switches Home to the DOM renderer for the rest of the session and preserves the selection.
  - Home mounts one extra card at each side of a row on Tizen and four on the web. WebGL's texture budget is 64 MB on TVs from 2020 onward and 40 MB on older or unidentified models. The renderer skips unchanged subtrees and steps only active animations.
  - On the same TV and in the same session, a first pass along a row measured 50–51 fps with WebGL and 29–37 fps with DOM. Holding Right measured 30–32 and 11–16 fps, respectively. The early experiments and their measurements are in the [WebGL spike plan](docs/plans/tv-gl-rows-plan.md), [initial row benchmarks](docs/development/tv-benchmarks.md#2026-11-17-home-rows-drawn-with-webgl-spike) and [PixiJS benchmarks](docs/development/tv-benchmarks.md#2026-11-17-home-rows-and-hero-drawn-with-pixijs-spike). The prototype is preserved on `tv-gl-spike`.
  - `scripts/tv-debug.mjs` opens the app over SDB and forwards DevTools to `127.0.0.1:9222`. `scripts/tv-bench.mjs` can serve local responses with `serve`, disable caching with `{"cache": false}` and stop a recording after the last key with `{"stop": true, "settle": ms}`. Renderer stats are available as `window.__glRows` in development or with `localStorage['miauflix:gl-tuning']` set on the TV. See the [rendering reference](docs/architecture/tv-gl-rendering.md), [production plan](docs/plans/tv-gl-production-plan.md) and [benchmarks](docs/development/tv-benchmarks.md).
- On Tizen, `MediaHero` fades details over 200 ms while you move and updates the backdrop after an 800 ms pause. The WebGL renderer preloads the new details after 100 ms and waits up to 600 ms for the logo before showing them. Artwork and progress updates for the same title keep the last complete details visible while the new ones load.
- Samsung TVs can play a remux when the original audio needs conversion. The [TV remux plan](docs/plans/tv-remux-plan.md) covers how media-load and the player fit together.
  - Media-load writes MPEG-TS HLS with the original video and one audio track per language. It copies compatible tracks and converts the others to AC-3 (640 kbit/s for 5.1, 448 kbit/s for three to five channels and 192 kbit/s for stereo or mono). It saves all subtitles as sidecars: WebVTT for text, Matroska with attachments for ASS/SSA, `.sup` for PGS and Matroska for DVD/DVB. AVPlay on the Tizen 5.5 test TV played HEVC 10-bit with AC-3 or AAC in MPEG-TS.
  - FFmpeg runs one job at a time and reads and uploads through loopback HTTP. Media-load encrypts playlists, segments and sidecars with AES-256-GCM under `<storage location>/remux/<id>/<keyed name>`. Playlists list only stored segments and close once the output is complete. Completion requires all listed segments and sidecars and a duration within 1.5 seconds of the plan. Jobs interrupted by a restart are queued again.
  - Media-load reserves space for the source size plus the estimated converted tracks before starting. It can reclaim lower-priority storage or skip the remux when space is tight. Download and remux allocations are accounted for separately as `MAX(allocatedBytes - remuxBytes, reservedBytes) + remuxBytes + remuxReservedBytes`. A remux a viewer is waiting for can interrupt background work as soon as its file finishes downloading.
  - Contract `media-load` 1.2 adds `requestRemux`, `remuxStatus`, `remuxChanged` and `readRemux`. Media-load migrations 4–7 add `remux`, `remux_file` and remux storage accounting. The backend's `AddMediaVariant` migration adds `media_variant` to track original and TV versions. `backfillMediaVariants` adds an original version for existing stored probes.
  - `TvRemuxService` uses the stored probe and the client's reported codecs to choose a compatible version. Playback can switch once the remux is complete or a minute ahead of the saved position. It keeps the last frame on screen during the switch. AVPlay opens at the intended position and buffers one second for HLS. The player requests the next episode's remux as soon as its download completes, checking once a minute for up to three hours.
  - `GET /api/stream/:token/remux/:name` limits requests to 600 per minute and forwards byte ranges for stored files. An unfinished playlist can be shortened on open with `?start=<seconds>` so AVPlay starts near the requested position. The full playlist is available after the first segment request.
  - The standalone `miauflix-media-load` image uses Debian (`oven/bun:1.4.0`, FFmpeg 7.1). On the test file, TrueHD conversion ran at about 19× real time on Debian, compared with about 3× on Alpine. Debian also loads the native `@thaunknown/utp-native` binary. The all-in-one image remains on Alpine.
- The [design system](docs/plans/style-guide.md) adds shared layout, type, color, density and focus tokens in `frontend/src/shared/design-system/`. The component catalog covers forms and native controls, tiles and artwork, navigation, status messages and progress, overlays, and playback controls. Storybook stories and tests cover the shared components and product patterns.
- The navigation layer in `shared/navigation` provides `NavigationProvider`, `FocusScope`, `FocusGroup`, `useFocusable` and `useComposite`. It moves focus through rows, grids and spatial layouts, keeps virtual and native focus in sync, and resumes a pending move when a group loads more content. Pointer, Tab and remote input use the same focus state. Dialogs and menus manage focus as they open and close.
- Sign-in, setup, title details, the player, provider settings, Home, Search and profile screens now use the design system. Tizen uses a 24 px root font above 1280 px and the 16 px web value at or below that width.
  - Sign-in and setup use `FormSection` and native fields. Remote input can enter and leave edit mode, move from email to password with Done and return to the password after an error. Tab reaches the password visibility toggle.
  - Title details put initial focus on the main action, show watchlist and rating labels on focus, and move from a season to its next episode. Episode rows include the synopsis and progress.
  - `PlaybackToolbar` places Play/Pause on the seek row. Left and Right seek there and while the controls are hidden. The controls stay visible while paused or while the subtitle panel is open. Up, Down or OK restores hidden controls. Mouse users can preview the seek position.
  - `HomeBrowse` uses `NavRail` and `Carousel`. Continue Watching takes initial focus when populated. Left from a row's first tile opens the rail, and Back toggles between it and the rows. Notifications, profile and Settings open as lists beside the rail. Settings links directly to Configuration, Storage, Sources and, on TV, the server picker.
  - Search uses the same wide `MediaTile` as Home. TV shows an on-screen keyboard and scrolling genre and history column. Other devices start in the search field. Returning from details restores the selected result.
  - Who's Watching shows profile tiles with their Plushies and an edit action under the focused tile. `ChoiceTileGroup` handles the profile editor's picture tiles and color swatches. The form scrolls to follow remote focus, and OK applies the choice. Back closes the TV keyboard before leaving the editor.

### Fixes

- Home's hero shade stays opaque up to the backdrop's left edge. Previously visited rows keep their cached cards when you scroll back quickly, and the final row can scroll to the same position under the hero as the others.
- Token-based TV sessions recover when a reload interrupts refresh-token rotation. Within `REFRESH_REUSE_GRACE_MS` (30 seconds), the previous token can list, refresh or sign out of the session. Recovery keeps the original rotation timestamp. Browser tabs continue to share a cookie and cannot refresh with the previous token.
- Home's hero (web, and TV without the WebGL rows): the band darkening the backdrop's bottom right is drawn again. An apostrophe in a CSS comment inside the hero's `::before` made styled-components' parser drop the following `::after` rule
- The notifications panel keeps loading additional pages when its scroll sentinel appears after the first render.
- Unstored list pages (`ListService.getOnDemandListPage`) failed with a 500 whenever any provider page after the first failed, even when the requested page already had its items: the count walk (`ON_DEMAND_COUNT_PAGES`) and the fill walk both rethrew. A failure once the page has items now ends the walk with a warning: the counting keeps the provider's estimated total, and a page cut short answers `total = start + items`, so the row (which pages by index, `homeFeed.middleware`) does not ask for a next page that would start past the entries it could not reach. With nothing to show yet, or when every entry in hand drops, the error still propagates, so a failure is never an empty page. Seen in the E2E stack, where AniList fixtures cover only page 1 and anime lists always drop entries (12 distinct shows on "All-Time Popular" page 1).
- Unstored list orders: a title in the catalog batch's `errors` (a transient failure, not `missing`) keeps its place in the remembered order, left out of that answer only, where it used to be dropped for the order's lifetime; only titles the catalog no longer returns at all leave the order. Resolution chunks and refetches go to the catalog in batches of at most 50 (`CATALOG_BATCH_LIMIT`). `onDemandOrders` drops expired orders on every request and keeps at most 200 lists (least recently used first), where an abandoned list's order stayed for the process lifetime. `getMediaByRefs` rebuilds its answer from the refs asked for, so a title asked twice comes back twice.
- Continue Watching now requests cards through authenticated `POST /api/media/batch` (up to 50 titles, five requests per second). `ListService.getMediaByRefs` loads them in one catalog call and returns them in request order. The app caches each title separately. A missing title fails its own lookup, and a 404 from an older server falls back to individual detail requests spaced 225 ms apart.
- The standalone media-catalog image (`services/media-catalog/Dockerfile`, `production`) bundled `sharp` without its native addon, so on start Bun installed `sharp` from the registry and crashed offline (`Could not load the "sharp" module`). `sharp` is now external to the bundle and ships with `@img/*`, `detect-libc` and `semver` in `/app/node_modules`; the combined `services/Dockerfile` images already carried `node_modules`.
- Vite resolves `@miauflix/service-contracts/card-image/v1` and `profiles/v1` from source, so tests and the dev server work from a clean checkout.
- Provider-backed Home rows page through Trakt and AniList results in order, filling gaps left by filtered episodes and unresolved titles. A later provider failure leaves the titles already loaded visible, and transient catalog errors preserve a title's position. The list service caches resolved order for five minutes and keeps up to 200 orders. A list that shrinks can return an empty page with the current total so Home can move back into range.
- Player status after a seek or track switch with converted audio: the player showed "Trying source N of M" again, left over from session creation, while the converted stream restarted at the new position on the same source. The source attempt now only shows while no session exists. Once a session plays, the backend reports the stage of a restarting converted stream with a new realtime message, `playback-stage` (`{ playbackId, stage: 'downloading' | 'converting' | 'ready' }`), sent only to the session that owns the playback. `openSourceInput` reports whether FFmpeg's latest read is waiting for bytes still being downloaded or receiving them. `AudioPlaybackService.stream` maps that to `downloading` / `converting` and sends `ready` with its first media; after that it reports nothing. A stage is reported only once it has lasted 400 ms (`stageReporter`), so FFmpeg's quick header and index reads do not make the message flicker. `ready` is always sent, which clears any stage left by a cancelled start. `PlaybackSessionService.reportStreamStage` finds the playback from its streaming key (live sessions now keep the key's hash), and the realtime gateway forwards it. The player shows "Downloading this part of …" or "Converting … for playback", and "Preparing … for playback" before any stage arrives
- Player: a source whose stream failed before its probe settled could hang the player on "Trying source N of M". The client holds a direct-stream error while `deliveryPending`, waiting for the background probe's verdict, but when the probe gave up (a permanent failure, or the last retry) the backend only logged "keeping direct delivery" and sent nothing, so the held error never resolved. Seen when a 4K source was refused for lack of storage (`source/greedy_download_failed`): no torrent started, so swarm health never failed it over either. The backend now sends a `playback-delivery` update of type `unprobed` when it stops inspecting, and the player settles the held failure as a direct-stream one, so it fails the source and asks for the next. A stream error that arrives after `unprobed` is not held either: nothing is pending once the probe gave up, so the source fails at once. Tests cover the update for a permanent inspection failure (none while a retry is pending) and the player moving on after it, whichever comes first.
- Fresh downloads pass WebTorrent an all-zero bitfield sized from the torrent metadata, allowing them to start without hashing an empty file. A new 43 GB download that repeatedly hit the 30-second attach timeout became ready in 16–21 ms. Existing bitfields are reused, and WebTorrent still verifies stored data when there is no recorded bitfield.
- Player: Resume Watching on an `audio-transcode` delivery stalled on "Trying source N of M" with an empty `<video>`. `useAudioDelivery` attached the MediaSource (`video.src = blob:`), then React detached the element's ref without removing it (`disappearLayoutEffects`, as a Suspense boundary hiding mounted content does), and `PlayerView`'s ref callback took that for an unmount: it removed the `blob:` src and called `load()`, so `sourceopen` never fired, no `/audio` request was made and the audio effect, whose dependencies had not changed, never ran again. The callback now releases the decoder in a microtask, and only for an element no longer connected to the page.
- Converted-audio playback copies the H.264 video through `-bsf:v h264_redundant_pps`. Blu-ray x264 encodes repeat their PPS with a different `pic_init_qp` (−14, −15 and 0 in the release here); Chrome's macOS VideoToolbox decoder failed on the change two to three seconds after every start (`PIPELINE_ERROR_DECODE … VTDecompressionOutputCallback`, `-12909`), the `<video>` errored and the player failed the source. ffmpeg decoded the same stream cleanly. The filter rewrites every PPS to one initial QP and moves the difference into each slice's `slice_qp_delta`, so the picture is bit-identical; reproduced and verified on 30 s clips in MSE. HEVC copy and the TV remux are unchanged.
- `assessSwarm` judges a swarm `healthy` whenever its download speed beats the bitrate (× 1.2, or × 0.6 while hedging) before applying the 25 s hard cap. A resume far into a 43 GB file could starve the viewer past the cap while downloading at 9 MB/s, get marked `failed`, recreate the session on another source and lose its storage to eviction.
- Playback checks each candidate with `playbackFits` (`media-load@1.3`). It counts pinned storage, space recoverable through eviction and 256 MiB of headroom for new allocation. It measures the movie's main video or the episode's own file, accounts for padding on version 2 stores and credits the verified bytes already on disk. Unknown sizes and older or unreachable media-load services fall through to normal admission. When no candidate fits, `POST /api/playback/sessions` returns 507 with `insufficient_storage` and the player offers Retry.
- Media-load deletes untracked entries under `DOWNLOAD_PATH` at startup and after adopting imported storage. Cleanup waits until the storage import is sealed and preserves the database, its SQLite sidecars and the allocation probe's temporary file. Set `DOWNLOAD_PATH_EXCLUSIVE=false` if the directory also holds other files.
- Samsung TVs no longer jump every few seconds while audio is converted: the stall watchdog took a moving playhead with no newly decoded frame for 1 s as a frozen picture and seeked in place (`frozen-seek`), but Tizen 5.5 updates `getVideoPlaybackQuality().totalVideoFrames` in batches of about 48 frames (2 s at 24 fps). A frozen picture now needs 5 s without a new frame.
- `CatalogRuntime` keeps one active catalog instance. A rejected configuration leaves the current instance serving, and successful activation retires the previous artwork worker and its queued background work. Artwork subscriptions survive the switch. Activation, deactivation and shutdown run in sequence.
- Logos the artwork worker cannot download (TMDB's SVG logos; only PNG is analyzed) were recorded as failed and retried every hour forever, republishing the title's artwork each time. The shared `isAnalyzableArtworkUrl` (`services/artwork-urls.ts`, also used by the worker) now lets `ArtworkSelectionService` skip them for good, and failures already recorded for them no longer schedule retries.
- Login locks an account for up to 15 minutes after 10 incorrect passwords. New passwords need at least eight characters, and sign-in takes the same password-checking path for unknown emails.
- Logout and refresh-token reuse after the 30-second grace period revoke the session, invalidate its access tokens and close its realtime sockets.
- Other security changes
  - Request bodies under `/api` are capped at 256 KiB (413 above it); the avatar upload keeps its own 1 MiB limit. Before, only the avatar route had a limit, and other endpoints, unauthenticated ones included, accepted whatever Bun allows by default.
  - New `SERVICE_TOKEN`: the backend presents it on every connection to the services (`RemoteService`, read through `ConfigurationService`), and the four services require it when it is set (the contract's `token` option). Without it, anyone reaching a service's port could use it and replace the `ticketKey` that signs `bytes` URLs. `docker-compose.yml` passes `SERVICE_TOKEN` to the backend and every service container. The services still listen on `0.0.0.0` by default, which their images rely on; the environment variable docs now say to set the token or `*_HOST=127.0.0.1` when running them natively, and that the token crosses the network unencrypted unless the service URL is `https://`.
  - `QrLoginService.create` stored the raw first `X-Forwarded-For` value as the requester's IP; it now uses `getRealClientIp`, which trusts the header only with the reverse proxy's secret.
  - The stream route logged failures with `console.error`, bypassing the logger's redaction; it uses `logger.error` now.
  - Streaming keys were cached for 90% of `STREAM_TOKEN_EXPIRATION` from their last verification, so a key verified shortly before it expired kept working for most of another lifetime. The cache entry now expires with the key.
  - `flaresolverr` is pinned to `v3.5.2` and `jaeger` (`jaegertracing/all-in-one`) to `1.76.0`, instead of `latest`.
- `hono` is updated from 4.10.6 to 4.13.18 (backend, frontend, backend-e2e) and `typeorm` from 0.3.27 to 0.3.31, for published advisories: among them arbitrary file access through `serveStatic` (which serves the web app), a `bodyLimit()` bypass with chunked bodies (used on avatar uploads) and cookie parsing and writing issues. `bun.lock` was also out of date (it still listed removed packages) and is regenerated.
- New audit entries retain only allowed request headers. Audit logs and traces redact stream and QR tokens in paths and mask sensitive query values, including in `Referer`. Existing audit rows retain their original contents.
- Requests that carry session cookies keep using HttpOnly cookies, even if they send `X-Auth-Transport: token`. The TV app still receives tokens in the response body.
- Responses now use Hono's `secureHeaders`, including a Content Security Policy with hashes for the web app's inline scripts. Additional artwork hosts can be listed as bare HTTP(S) origins in `CSP_IMAGE_ORIGINS`. Caddy sets `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000`.
- New downloads use version 2 of the encrypted chunk store. Each store has random `contentSalt` and `filenameSalt` values, sealed in its layout, and a random directory. HMAC-SHA256 derives a separate AES-256-CTR key for each store and names each file from its full path. Counters track the byte offset in 16-byte blocks. This removes keystream reuse across downloads and removes `DOWNLOAD_SALT` from new storage. Version 1 layouts and existing download locations stay readable in place.
- `STORAGE_PAD_FILE_SIZES` defaults to `true` for version 2 downloads. The chunk store adds random bytes before the first write and counts the padded size in reservations. Files up to 64 KiB occupy 64 KiB. Larger files grow by at most 3.1%, or 1.6% from 4 GiB. An incomplete download on a sparse filesystem can still expose its exact size through the extent map, and the media-load database retains exact lengths and offsets.
- Server and media-load loggers replace magnet links and matching info hashes with `[redacted]`, including in error messages, stacks and causes. Source-related messages use the source ID.
- Server failures reached users as a generic message with no way to find the cause. A TMDB failure inside media-catalog (a rejected token, a 5xx, a timeout) became the contract's `internal` / "Internal service error", the backend's realtime search answered only `search-error` / `search-failed` ("Search is unavailable right now"), and HTTP 500 bodies put the text in `message` while the frontend read only `error`, so every 500 showed the caller's fallback text.
  - Contract: `ServiceOptions.mapError` turns a known non-contract failure into the `ContractError` callers receive (mapped failures are still reported through `onError`). media-catalog maps TMDB `ProviderError`s (`mapProviderError`): 401/403 become `not_configured` ("TMDB rejected the API access token…"), timeouts `timeout`, 429 `unavailable`, other statuses `internal` with TMDB's status in the message. A call before the data plane is attached fails as `not_configured` instead of a plain `Error`.
  - Backend: `ServiceNotConfiguredError` gains an optional `reason`, filled from the remote service's `not_configured` message. A remote `unavailable` (not connected, TMDB rate limiting) is now a separate `ServiceUnavailableError` (503 `service_unavailable`, with a retry hint and no `needsConfiguration`) instead of being reported as unconfigured. New `describeError` (`errors/error-response.ts`) maps any thrown value to a status and an `ApiErrorBody` (`routes/error.types.ts`, exported to the frontend): `success: false`, `status`, a stable `code` (`service_not_configured`, `unauthorized`, `service_timeout`, `service_internal`, `<type>/<code>` for `AppError`s, `internal`…), the readable text in both `error` and `message`, an 8-hex `errorId`, and for configuration problems `needsConfiguration`, `service`, `serviceName` and a `hint` naming the setting to fix (TMDB for the catalog, Trakt for lists). ### Improvements

- Playback ranking gives sources that recently streamed well a boost among otherwise similar candidates. The boost fades over time.
- Browsers render styled ASS/SSA tracks with JASSUB, including the release's bundled fonts. `GET /api/subtitles/tracks/:candidateId?format=ass` serves the track in its original timing. The authenticated `/fonts` and `/fonts/:fontId` routes list and serve attachments. The backend extracts fonts together and caps them at 32 MB per font and 96 MB per video. The browser loads them as they arrive and falls back to WebVTT if JASSUB fails. The Tizen build uses WebVTT. Plain subtitles on browsers and AVPlay sit near the bottom as outlined text and move up when controls are open. The player uses chapter markers for Skip intro, Skip credits and the next-episode countdown, which waits for any post-credits scene.
- Episodes are searched again at most once every six hours if no saved single-file match has at least 10 seeders (`WEAK_SWARM_BROADCASTERS`).
- **Releases made for another language rank last**: `ContentSource` gains a nullable `languages` column (schema sync; comma-separated `Language` values), filled from the candidate's parsed `language` and refreshed like the other provider fields whenever a search sees the release again; existing rows stay null (unknown, not demoted) until then. `utils/release-language.util.ts` treats a release as made for another language when its name gives a dub or local-subtitle language (French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Czech, Slovak) and neither one of the viewer's languages nor English; MULTI and original-language tags (Japanese) never count. The viewer's languages are the primary subtags of the playback request's `audioLanguages` (the browser's `navigator.languages`) plus the subtitle language, English when none is given. `PlayablePreparationService.sortCandidates` moves such releases after all others (after the bitrate-cap ordering), and `select` looks for a requested quality among the others first. Before, two well-seeded 1080p copies of Tokyo Revengers S4E4 tied on streaming score and a French Tsundere-Raws release (VF/VOSTFR) won. - Tracker scrapes are batched (`services/media-load/src/scrape/scrape-batcher.ts`, in the media-load service below). Requests made within 50 ms of each other share one multi-hash scrape per tracker group: up to 70 hashes in one UDP packet and 20 per HTTP request (about 1,400 URL characters). Previously each hash created its own `bittorrent-tracker` client. Concurrent requests for the same hash share one scrape, including requests that arrive after it was sent, and results are reused for 60 seconds (expired ones are swept at most once a minute). UDP trackers are given raw hash bytes and HTTP trackers hex strings in separate clients: `bittorrent-tracker` 11 packs an array of hex strings for UDP as ASCII text (40 bytes per hash), so a single shared client would send malformed UDP scrapes. `ws://` and `wss://` scrape trackers are ignored. Counts are now the highest seeders and leechers any tracker reported, not the first tracker's answer. Each hash settles on its own, when every tracker has answered it or 1 second after its first reply. A hash no tracker answers within 5 seconds still fails with `scrape_timeout`. v2 (64-hex) hashes are scraped by their first 20 bytes (BEP 52). The scrape timer is cleared when a scrape settles, and stopping the service fails waiting and in-flight scrapes at once with `scrape_unavailable`, without counting them as failed. - Torrent priorities. WebTorrent now comes from the `maury91/webtorrent` fork. The fork adds a shared `connectionBudget`, `torrent.setPriority()`, per-torrent `throttleDownloadSpeed`/`throttleUploadSpeed`, BEP 40 peer priority and uTP over IPv6. It replaces `utp-native` with `@thaunknown/utp-native`. The new `CONTENT_CONNECTION_BUDGET` setting (default 200) is the connection total shared by all torrents. Each torrent's share is its priority divided by the sum of every torrent's priority. Once the budget is used up, a torrent at its share takes no new peers, and no torrent loses peers it already has. `CONTENT_CONNECTION_LIMIT` stays a per-torrent limit. `DownloadService` sets priorities from `torrent-priority.ts`:
  - 10 while a file is streamed
  - otherwise the strongest live claim: playback 6, warmup 4, prefetch or watched 2, watchlist 1
  - 1 for seeding with no claim

  A torrent starts at its claim's priority. A playback start that joins a background start already in flight raises it. While a stream is still buffering (the existing speculative pause), torrents without a viewer are each capped at a quarter of `CONTENT_DOWNLOAD_LIMIT`. With no global limit (or one too large to be a finite number) they are not capped. Priorities are recomputed when a torrent's files become known, when claims are reconciled, when a stream opens or closes, and when the stream finishes buffering, with or without paused speculative downloads. Calls that arrive during a recompute are folded into one more pass.

### Bug fixes

- TV discovery falls back to detected addresses when `TV_PAIRING_ADDRESSES` is unset.
- Playback probes list streams without dumping attachment data. This keeps large bundled fonts from cutting the probe short before it finds the duration, audio tracks and chapter markers. `Ending` is recognized as a credits marker.
- An episode's first play resolves up to two candidates' torrent files in parallel, so it waits for at most one 15-second lookup timeout. If both slots are busy, the lookup can wait for a slot and try once more.
- On older Samsung TVs (Tizen 5.5), notification and episode rows use flex layout with fixed-size icons and posters.
- A source that failed during playback stopped downloading only when the playback ended. `PlaybackSessionService.failOver` moved the viewers to a new session but kept their `watched` claim (owner `user:<userId>:<playable>`) on the failed source, so media-load went on downloading it greedily next to the replacement; on the VPS a 4K source took about 5 MB/s of the 20 MB/s available after it had been dropped. Fail-over now releases that claim (`releaseClaims(sourceId, ownerKey, 'watched')`, once per viewer), and media-load detaches the torrent once nothing else holds it. What was downloaded stays on disk.
- Storage pressure counted downloads nobody claimed at their full size. Every download reserves its whole file (`reservedBytes`) before it starts, and storage is charged `MAX(allocatedBytes, reservedBytes)`, but the reservation stayed after its claims were gone: a warmup or preload that fetched a few MB of a 13 GB torrent kept charging 13 GB. On the VPS such reservations added about 180 GB to the 100 GB actually on disk, so starting a 39 GB 4K source evicted 22 stored downloads. media-load now releases a storage's reservation (`StorageService.releaseReservation`, measuring its allocation first) when its torrent is detached because nothing claims or streams it, and a new claim reserves again before it downloads. Media-load migration 3 clears the reservation of existing storage with no claim and no active stream where it exceeds the allocation
- Audio inspection of a playing source is retried after 5 and 15 seconds when it fails (not for a 422, which a retry cannot change), while the playback session is still live, and the embedded subtitle probe is tried once more after 3 seconds (not for speculative probes). Both used to give up at the first failure, which on the VPS hit all four probes of a source playback had just switched to, leaving audio unconverted and embedded subtitles unlisted. FFmpeg's stderr was discarded; the last 2 KB now goes into the logs with the failure (`stderr-tail.ts`; `AudioPlaybackError.detail`, never sent to the client)
- Unexpected errors reaching the backend's error handler are logged with the request method, path (long token-like segments masked as `:token`) and stack, instead of only the message; a `TypeError: immutable` seen twice on the VPS during a source switch could not be traced without them
- The services container's fallback access check on the downloads folder (run when changing its owner fails) skips symbolic links, as `chown -R` does: `-d`/`-f` followed them, so a stray link to something read-only stopped every service from starting. media-load never creates links there.

### Features

- Source search moves to the Bun sources service (`services/sources`, port 3003). It validates candidate hashes, merges trackers and returns results as they arrive. Episode search falls back from IMDb to episode and paginated season queries so older episodes and season packs can turn up.
- Settings → Sources lets admins enable, reorder and test providers, set request budgets and manage definition repositories. The admin-only `/api/sources` API uses sources contract 1.1. JSON manifests are capped at 256 KB and refreshed daily. Password settings are encrypted at rest and masked in the UI.
- Runtime provider management (sources contract 1.1): admins can enable, disable and reorder providers, override per-provider daily and per-minute request budgets, test a provider, and add, refresh or remove provider repositories (JSON manifests, 256 KB cap, refreshed daily; the built-in `builtin:miauflix` repository cannot be removed and a repository never overwrites a provider owned by another one). Provider settings are sealed at rest and password-type values are masked. The backend exposes this under admin-only `/api/sources`, and the frontend adds Settings → Sources. Repository entries of kind `http` are stored but only run once that provider type ships.
- Engine compatibility with community definitions (found by trying [dreulavelle/Prowlarr-Indexers `Custom`](https://github.com/dreulavelle/Prowlarr-Indexers/tree/main/Custom), a set of aggregator definitions that all search by IMDb id): the short `caps.categories` form, JSONPath roots (`$.data.items`) in JSON selectors, namespaced XML selectors (`torznab|attr[name=infohash]`), and no CSS check on selectors of JSON definitions. Most of those definitions need a debrid account or a self-hosted instance, so a `torrentio.yml` for the public Torrentio addon was added to `maury91/miauflix-indexers` (one request by IMDb id and season/episode returns episodes and season packs); it is one of the built-in providers, switched on.
- The indexer picker's filters now describe indexers that need an account too: what their definition says they can do (IMDb search, movies, TV, anime) is recorded even though they cannot be installed, so IMDb search with Public only off lists the 244 definitions that declare it (4 of them public) instead of only the ones we can run. Their rows say why they cannot be added and still show what they support. Stored rows are re-read from the cached definitions once, without downloading anything.
- **No indexer-specific code in the repository.** The native YTS, EZTV and TheRARBG providers, their recorded fixtures, mirror discovery, the site mocks and the `yts-sanitizer` and `therarbg-sanitizer` packages are removed, along with the `YTS_API_URL` and `THE_RARBG_API_URL` settings. Every indexer is a Cardigann definition fetched at runtime. By default that is Prowlarr's `definitions/v11` (YTS among others) plus `maury91/miauflix-indexers`, a separate repository holding the definitions Prowlarr lacks or that do not search by IMDb id (EZTV's JSON API, TheRARBG by title and by IMDb id). A fresh install enables TheRARBG (IMDb), YTS, EZTV (API), Torrentio and TheRARBG (title), and lists Nyaa, EZTV and The Pirate Bay switched off; migration removes the old native provider rows and their usage. A source's own description is shown on its row (`ProviderView.description`), and the backend E2E suite now uses a generic indexer mock with its own test definitions, loaded through the new `SOURCES_PRELOAD_PROVIDERS` startup setting (`<definition id>@<source URL>` entries).
- Jackett Cardigann engine (sources contract 1.2): the sources service runs public Jackett YAML definitions natively, without Jackett. Definitions are fetched at runtime from the configured definition sources (see below; Jackett's are GPL-2.0, so they are never bundled), cached in the service database and kept current by the hourly sync. The supported subset covers Go templates (`if`/`else`/`range`, `or`/`and`/`eq`/`ne`/`not`/`re_replace`/`join`), the usual text, date and size filters, and HTML, JSON (including nested rows and `..` parent access) and XML selectors. Definitions that need an account, use an unsupported filter or template feature, or are malformed are rejected at install time. They remain visible in the indexer picker with the reason they cannot be added, unless excluded by the Public only or Hide unsupported filters. Results that only offer a `.torrent` link or a details page are resolved to a hash by reading the best-seeded eight per search; the rest are dropped. Three public indexers (Nyaa, EZTV, The Pirate Bay) are listed in the built-in repository, disabled; enabling one fetches its definition.
- Torznab client: add a Jackett or Prowlarr endpoint with its URL and API key (sealed at rest). Capabilities come from `t=caps` (cached 24 h, with defaults when unreachable); IMDb searches try `tt…` first and fall back to digits once.
- The Jackett definition list is synced into the sources service database every hour (`CARDIGANN_SYNC_INTERVAL_MINUTES`, default 60; the first run starts shortly after the service is configured, and failed runs retry after 5 minutes). For GitHub-hosted definitions a run uses git (a shallow, sparse, blob-less clone of the definitions folder in the service's data directory; `git` is installed in the service images): one fetch reports which files changed and one checkout downloads only those in a single pack. Measured against Jackett's repository, the first sync takes about 4 seconds and a run with no changes about 1 second, where one request per file took over 35 seconds. If git is unavailable or cannot reach GitHub, a run falls back to listing the upstream tree through GitHub's trees API and downloading the files one by one (set `SOURCES_USE_GIT=false` to always do so). That listing is compact and not capped at 1,000 files, unlike the contents API and compares each file's sha with what it stored: an unchanged tree sha downloads nothing, otherwise only added or changed definitions are downloaded again (6 at a time) and removed ones are dropped, with the change counts kept (`lastChange`). Sources without per-file versions (`<url>/index.json`) are re-read daily instead. Each definition is read once into a `cardigann_index` table (name, language, type, IMDb/TMDB/TVDB search, movies, TV, anime, FlareSolverr, supported or why not), and installed indexers pick up their new definition automatically. A manual Check for updates starts a run at once (sources contract 1.3).
- The indexer definitions come from a configurable list of sources, `CARDIGANN_DEFINITION_SOURCES` (GitHub folder URLs, as `…/tree/<ref>/<path>` or the raw form, separated by spaces or commas; any other http(s) folder that serves an `index.json` also works). Existing data is migrated: cached definitions and the catalog keep their Jackett origin.

The backend retains candidate persistence, episode-file matching, WebTorrent metadata, tracker stats, notifications and playback; provider metadata-file URLs cross the boundary only as encrypted references.

On-demand movie searches keep saving later results after the caller's response timeout.

A user can have up to 200 pending requests, for titles the catalog knows. Requests are re-checked in the background every 6 hours (every pending request, not only the 50 oldest, and a title whose check fails doesn't stop the others), and an episode's matches that are not verified yet are queued for a check

### Improvements

- The TV's Home hero backdrop is sized for the TV, and sharpens while you rest on a title. It used a `w780` backdrop on every TV, which a 2023 QCQ80 stretched to the hero's 1344 pixels. Now the backdrop shown while moving and the one shown once the selection rests are each the smallest size that covers the hero (70% of the app's width), up to what the TV's generation paints smoothly (`frontend/src/shared/config/tv-profile.ts`): 2023 and newer TVs, dated by their model code (`webapis.productinfo.getModelCode()`, `23_…`) or else their browser version, get `w1280` while moving and the `original` at rest (measured on a QCQ80: 70 ms and 97 ms to decode); older ones `w780` and `w1280`. Once the selection rests, the hero loads the resting backdrop while idle (no key pressed for a second), decoded and ahead of the rows' idle preloads, and crossfades to it; moving on drops it unless it has started. A title whose resting backdrop has loaded shows it at once, and details uses it too. `MIAUFLIX_TV_HERO_BACKDROP` and `MIAUFLIX_TV_HERO_BACKDROP_RESTING` (`w300`, `w780`, `w1280`, `original` or `auto`) set the sizes when packaging (`docs/setup/samsung-tv.md`)
- Home on the TV: Back on the remote (or Esc) while browsing the rows opens the sidebar, and pressing it again returns to the selected card. Right on the sidebar's bell no longer opens an empty notifications panel when there are no notifications, read or unread
- Playback can be capped by bitrate: a playback session's `preferences.maxBitrateKbps` (500 to 1,000,000) is the highest average bitrate the client's connection sustains. A movie's sources are estimated from their size and the movie's runtime: the ones within the cap keep their ranking and are tried first, then the rest from the leanest, so a movie with nothing under the cap still plays. A finished download over the cap no longer wins over a leaner torrent. Episodes are capped too, against the show's typical episode length (TMDB's shortest episode runtime, stored on the show as `episodeRuntime` on its next details sync) and the size of the episode's own file in the source, so a season pack counts one episode, not the whole season. A file stored by its path inside the provider's release (without the torrent's top folder) is found by that suffix, as playback does, when exactly one file matches; with several matches its size stays unknown. A show without an episode runtime on TMDB stays uncapped, and a source whose file list is unknown keeps its place. A requested quality (rather than `auto`) is matched among the sources within the cap first, so asking for 4K no longer picks a 4K source over the cap when a lighter source fits; over-cap sources are only used when nothing fits The TV app asks for 8 Mbit/s at most: it fetches a stream at about 11 Mbit/s, and a 4K remux (~35 Mbit/s) froze on its first frame for 18 seconds, then played at a third of real time without audio
- On the TV, loading spinners no longer keep the main thread busy: Chromium 69 animates SVG (the spinner icon's own SMIL loop and its CSS spin) on the main thread, a full frame on every display refresh even out of view, so a row still loading far below the screen cost about 10 ms per frame for as long as it loaded. The TV build spins an HTML element instead, which the compositor animates, including the hero's "checking sources" indicator, and a row loading off screen shows no spinner.
- Home no longer re-renders when the backend pushes the focused title's source status or warmup progress (every few hundred milliseconds while a title warms up): the hero and details read it themselves, and the hero ignores warmup progress, which it doesn't show. Rows and the hero no longer re-render when their own promotion and backdrop requests start and finish, and the set of empty lists keeps its identity while it doesn't change, so loading pages no longer re-renders Home. On the TV a single move along a row now takes 42–44 ms of main thread, down from 47–66.
- Home loads its lists from the store instead of from each row: Home names the rows it wants, a middleware keeps the list pages they need (the selected page and the pages beside it) subscribed and releases the rest, and rows only read the cache. Empty lists are left out before their row renders, so no row mounts just to find out it is empty.
- Home on the TV readies the rows below while you're idle: from a second after the last key press, one more row every half second, up to 10 rows below the visible ones. A ready row is fetched, mounted and rendered in full off screen, so moving down finds it ready; it isn't promoted to the backend until it is on screen. Home also scrolls between rows with a CSS transition that the compositor runs, and the rows around the focused one mount, unmount and load once the slide to it has ended, not during it or on every move of a quick run.

### Security

- The database no longer names what is stored or watched in plain text: file paths inside sources (movie and episode files, stored files and download claims), ffprobe output, recent searches and notification titles and messages are encrypted at rest with the existing source key, in columns renamed to names that do not describe them (`k`, `v`, `a`, `b`). Existing databases are converted by data migrations at the first start (renamed in place before the schema sync, then encrypted, safe to rerun), and the file is rewritten with `VACUUM` so the replaced plain text does not stay readable in freed pages. Links between tables (which movie a source belongs to, which source is stored, playback progress) are still readable to someone who knows the schema

- The frontend dev and preview servers (Vite) check the certificate of the backend they proxy `/api` to, unless `BACKEND_URL` is local (`localhost`, a `127.x.x.x` or `[::1]` address, or one of the compose service names `backend`, `miauflix` and `vpn`). Pointing `BACKEND_URL` at a remote HTTPS backend accepted any certificate before

- Home sends the focused title and the cards in view to the new socket after switching sessions, instead of waiting for the next move. Artwork pushed just before Home closes no longer comes back after Home removed it. A viewport promotion that failed is retried the next time the same cards are in view
- Home no longer stays hidden behind the logo for tens of seconds on a slow device (seen on the 4K e2e run): the intro started only once the browser had an idle moment, which a device busy drawing every frame may not have for a long time. It now waits at most 0.5 s for an idle moment once it is ready. On the TV it also waits for the page's images to load and for frames to run smoothly, at most 5 s
- The logo animation visual test (`e2e/logo-animation.storybook.spec.ts`) no longer fails with `window.seek is not a function` on a cold Storybook load: it waits for the story to expose `window.seek` instead of relying on `networkidle`, which could fire before the story rendered
- Compose runs [autoheal](https://github.com/willfarrell/docker-autoheal) to restart the backend after three consecutive health-check failures. The check now uses `/api/health` and allows eight seconds per request.
- The server no longer becomes slow (pages taking 20 s or more, health checks timing out) after running for a few hours. WebTorrent 2.8 opened a new UDP socket with its own buffers for every outgoing uTP peer attempt, and did not count those attempts against the connection limit, so with tens of thousands of discovered peers the backend spent most of its CPU allocating buffers and collecting garbage, and grew to about 3 GB of buffer memory. WebTorrent is upgraded to 3.0.21, which sends all uTP connections through the single listening socket and keeps attempts within the connection limit (2000 unreachable peers: 6000 sockets and 540 MB of buffers before, 1 socket and 15 MB after). WebTorrent 3 also accepts encrypted peer connections by default
- A stream with no video returns 404. If every candidate is exhausted, session creation returns 404 with `reason: "exhausted"`. New-episode notifications require a source verified to contain video.
- Playback no longer stops with "The media buffer is full and playback is not advancing" on high-bitrate files using the audio-converting stream. On a full buffer the player removed media up to 1 s behind the playhead, but the browser extends a removal to the next keyframe, so this also removed the frames being played and froze playback. The player now only lowers its read-ahead limit and waits, and lets the browser evict what is behind the playhead. It reports a stall after about 10 s without progress instead of 2 s
- Playback on the audio-converting stream no longer freezes at small gaps in the media: in open-GOP encodes (x265's default) the 2–3 frames shown just before each keyframe are decoded after it, and Chrome often reports them as a gap in the buffered video. Chrome froze the picture there while audio played on, then stalled the whole player a few seconds later until a seek. A watchdog now jumps gaps of up to 1 s just before the playhead reaches them, and as a fallback re-seeks when the playhead stops for about 1 s with media buffered ahead, or keeps moving while no video frame is decoded
- Show Settings in the sidebar only to admin users. Keyboard and remote navigation skip it as well, so a non-admin can no longer select the hidden button with ArrowDown and open it with Enter

- Connecting a Trakt account now ends cleanly when the user denies access, the code expires or was already used: the dialog stops with that outcome instead of a server error. Trakt asking the app to poll more slowly no longer fails the connection either
- A backend e2e run no longer overwrites a customized `backend-e2e/.env.test` with a leftover backup; it restores only the backup it made itself, and the backup file is ignored by git
- TV sign-in starts with the email field focused. Up and Down move between fields and Continue, Done moves to the password, and a failed login returns to it. Right from the password opens the visibility toggle, then reaches Get a new code when the QR code expires.
- Files whose default audio a player can't decode, but which carry a decodable track in the same language, now play as they are on players that switch tracks themselves (the Samsung TV), instead of converting the audio. For example a 4K release with an English TrueHD default and an English AC-3 track used to be converted, which on a large source could time out ("Audio conversion timed out"); it now plays the AC-3 track directly. Without a decodable track in that language the audio is still converted, rather than switching to another language
- The TV player takes focus when it opens and moves it to Retry or Notify me on an error. Retrying restores focus to the player so Back keeps working.
- Find subtitles and watchlist buttons hold focus while requests run. When a control disappears or loses focus, the first direction key restores the last control or the page's default through `onRestoreFocus`.
- Tizen 5.5 uses smaller card images and preloads nearby artwork during idle time. The hero, rows and intro animate on separate layers so updating a title doesn't repaint the screen.

## 2026-10-26 v0.0.2 Streaming reliability and HTTPS fixes

### Fixes

- Playback ranks sources by streaming score. Scores update in the same transaction as source and peer-count changes, so a simultaneous provider refresh and stats update can't leave an old score behind. The first data migration backfills existing sources.
- The realtime connection (live playback updates, notifications, search) works behind HTTPS: `docker-compose.https.yml` sets `CORS_ORIGIN` to the domain, since the default only allows `localhost`
- HTTPS and the VPN can be combined: the `Caddyfile` forwards to `MIAUFLIX_UPSTREAM` (`vpn:3000` with `docker-compose.vpn.yml`)
- The player no longer shows a blue rectangle when its controls overlay takes focus; the volume slider uses the same white focus ring as the buttons
- Video no longer buffers constantly behind HTTPS for viewers on a VPN: the `Caddyfile` serves HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2 only and `docker-compose.https.yml` no longer publishes 443/UDP. Over HTTP/3 both the direct and the audio-converting stream reached the browser at 4–5 Mbit/s; over HTTP/2 they reach about 150 Mbit/s
- Playback no longer fails with "The SourceBuffer is full" when the audio-converting stream arrives faster than it plays: the player limits read-ahead in seconds, but the browser's media buffer is limited in bytes (about 150 MB in Chrome), so 60 s of a high-bitrate file could overflow it. On a full buffer the player now evicts what is behind the playhead, lowers its read-ahead limit (to no less than 10 s), waits for playback and retries, instead of stopping. While paused it waits for playback to resume; if playback runs but neither the playhead nor eviction moves for 8 checks in a row (about 2 s), it reports the error instead of retrying forever
